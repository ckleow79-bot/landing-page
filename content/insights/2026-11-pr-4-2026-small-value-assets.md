## What changed

On 30 September 2026 LHDN published Public Ruling No. 4/2026, which replaces Public Ruling No. 3/2021. The limits under paragraph 19A, Schedule 3 ITA 1967 (RM2,000 per item; RM20,000 a year for non-MSMEs and LLPs) have applied since YA 2020. In the update, the ruling now uses "micro, small and medium companies" (MSMEs, *SMKS*), points to Public Ruling No. 8/2025 for who qualifies, adds LLPs to the definitions and updates its examples.

## The story: a restaurant goes shopping

Picture a restaurant and catering company. LHDN's own example calls it Smarts Kitchen Sdn Bhd. In YA 2025 it buys 13 items: chairs, tables, two air-conditioners, a vacuum cleaner, a microwave, two convection ovens, a dish cabinet, a dish dryer and a set of crockery. Total: RM26,050.

The owner asks one question: **"Can I write all of this off this year?"** The ruling answers it in five steps.

**Step 1: each item, RM2,000 or less, and not on hire purchase.** One convection oven cost RM3,000. Out. It gets normal capital allowances.

**Step 2: it must last at least two years.** The crockery set (RM2,000) does not. Out. Crockery is dealt with on the *replacement basis*: the first purchase is not deductible; replacements are deductible under s.33(1)(c).

**Step 3: check the annual cap.** That leaves RM21,050.
- If the company is an **MSME**: no cap. All RM21,050 gets a 100% special allowance this year.
- If it is **not an MSME, or it is an LLP**: the cap is RM20,000 a year. In LHDN's example the company takes the RM1,800 microwave out, claims RM19,250 as special allowance and claims normal capital allowances on the microwave and the RM3,000 oven.

**Step 4 and 5: claim normal capital allowances on whatever was taken out, and the special allowance on the rest.**

## Three plot twists

1. **Investment holding companies.** An unlisted investment holding company is treated as having no business income, so it is not an MSME for this rule. The ruling also says it does not meet the general conditions for capital allowances. (Example 3 in the ruling.)
2. **Selling a written-off item.** The gym in LHDN's Example 4 claimed 100% on a RM1,300 machine and later sold it for RM800. That RM800 comes back as a balancing charge.
3. **Choose once.** If you claim normal capital allowances on these assets, you must stay with that until the expenditure is fully absorbed. If you claim the special allowance on an asset, you cannot switch that asset back.

## Key points

1. Each item must cost **RM2,000 or less**, be used in the business, last **at least two years** and **not be on hire purchase**.
2. **MSMEs: no annual cap.** Other companies and LLPs: **RM20,000 a year**. Any excess gets normal capital allowances.
3. Whether you are an MSME depends on Public Ruling No. 8/2025, including the ownership condition from YA 2024.
4. Sell a written-off item and the proceeds can come back as a balancing charge.
5. Keep a register that shows which assets got the special allowance, and keep records for 7 years (s.82A).
