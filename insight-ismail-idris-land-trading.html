150 lots, 11 years, RM44 million: investment or trade? (Ismail bin Idris v KPHDN)

Keith Leow, Licensed Tax Agent · 6 November 2026 · information as at 6 November 2026

The Penang High Court agreed with LHDN that a RM44 million sale of 150 land lots held for 11 years was business income, not a capital gain. Holding land for a long time did not settle it.

The story in four scenes

Scene 1: 2004. The purchase. A director of a construction company buys 150 lots in Mukim Pengkalan Kundor, Kota Setar, Kedah, from that same company for RM850,000. The company had already subdivided the land and converted it from agricultural to building land.

Scene 2: 2004 to 2015. The long wait. Eleven years pass. No building, no improvements, no marketing, and no income from the land.

Scene 3: March 2015. The sale. All 150 lots go to PR1MA Corporation Berhad for RM44,000,000. The seller reports the sale for real property gains tax on Form CKHT 1A. In his view the story ends here: a long-term investment that grew in value.

Scene 4: five years later. The twist. After an audit, LHDN raises an additional assessment for YA 2015. It treats the profit as business income under s.4(a) ITA 1967, not a capital gain. Tax plus a 45% penalty under s.112(3), for failing to furnish a return, totals RM15,637,352.68. The Special Commissioners dismissed his appeal on 1 March 2024. On 23 October 2026 the High Court in Penang dismissed it too, with RM12,000 costs.

Why "I held it for 11 years" was not enough

Courts decide "investment or trade?" by looking at the whole picture, using what are called the badges of trade. Think of them as the clues in a detective story. No single clue convicts; the pattern does.

According to LHDN's summary, the clues it relied on included:

  • How much, and what kind: 150 lots, already approved for building, is a lot of land for one private investor.
  • Where: a strategic location.
  • The numbers: bought at RM850,000, sold at RM44 million.
  • Who: the buyer was a director of the company that subdivided and converted the land, with experience in construction, development and property through that company.
  • Holding period: 11 years, but with no income at all from the land in that time. A long hold with no rent looks less like an investment.

The taxpayer argued that he did nothing to the land, that the conversion happened before he bought it, that his experience was in construction rather than property development, and that he had already declared the sale for RPGT.

"But I already declared it for RPGT"

This is the line many readers will recognise. LHDN's answer, which the court accepted: no RPGT assessment was ever raised on the sale, so filing Form CKHT 1A did not stop LHDN from taxing the profit under the Income Tax Act. It also said the additional assessment was raised within the time limit in s.91(1).

In plain terms: telling LHDN about a sale is not the same as LHDN agreeing how it should be taxed.

Key points

  1. A long holding period helps, but it is not a shield. LHDN and the courts look at all the facts together.
  2. Your background counts. A buyer connected to construction or development faces closer questions about "investment" land.
  3. Buying many units or lots, especially ones already approved for building, looks more like stock than an investment.
  4. Filing an RPGT return does not settle the matter if LHDN later decides the gain is business income.
  5. Penalties can be heavy. Here LHDN imposed 45% of the tax under s.112(3), for failing to furnish a return.
  6. Keep evidence of your intention at the time of purchase (board minutes, financing, plans, rental efforts). It is far easier to keep it now than to rebuild it in an audit years later.

Where this stands

This note is based on LHDN's published summary (ReveNews). The full written judgment was not available when we wrote it. The taxpayer may appeal to the Court of Appeal within 30 days of the decision. General information only, not advice on your case.
