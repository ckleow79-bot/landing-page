An audit, in five scenes

What happens when the tax audit notice arrives?

  1. 1

    The letter nobody wants to open.

    A tax audit notice lands on the taxpayer's desk. The clock starts the day it arrives, and somewhere in the office a shoebox starts to sweat.

  2. 2

    The tax officer asks: "May I see your records?"

    Out comes the shoebox. Every business has one. Receipts escape, the paper tower leans, and one invoice sticks to the taxpayer.

  3. 3

    The numbers that don't explain themselves.

    Large cash deposits, the director's current account, travel without receipts. This is usually where the tax officer's questions begin.

  4. 4

    Your tax agent steps in.

    B & K rebuilds the records, reconciles the bank to the books, and prepares a position paper with the authority behind every point.

  5. 5

    The file closes. You breathe again.

    The tax officer has the answers, the taxpayer has tidy records, and the shoebox retires. Outcomes depend on the facts; preparation is the part you control.

One-minute self-check

Would your records survive an audit?

Six quick questions. Your answers stay in your browser and are not sent anywhere.

  1. Do you keep a receipt or invoice for every business expense?
  2. Is your bank statement reconciled to your accounts every month?
  3. Can you explain every large cash deposit into the business account?
  4. Are director's loans and withdrawals documented, not just "in the director's account"?
  5. Do your e-Invoices and sales records match the income you declare?
  6. Do you keep records for at least seven years after the year of assessment?
